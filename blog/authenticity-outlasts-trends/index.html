Authenticity outlasts trends: narrative over FOMO in AI content

3 Aug 2026 · 6 min read · GPT3 Marketing editorial team · FAQ

Authenticity outlasts trends because trends reward speed, which everyone now has, while a consistent narrative rewards recognition, which compounds over time. With AI making trend content almost free to produce, brands that chase every format blur together. Brands that filter trends through a clear narrative and real audience evidence stay distinctive and earn lasting trust.

Why does trend chasing feel necessary?

Our listening on brand differentiation surfaces a familiar pressure: fear of missing out. A format takes off, competitors pile in, and the team worries that sitting it out means invisibility. Generative AI intensifies this. A trend post that once took a day now takes minutes, so the cost of joining seems negligible.

The hidden cost is identity. When every brand uses the same audio, the same caption structure and the same model to write it, the audience remembers the trend, not the brand.

What does the evidence from audiences suggest?

Across SOMIN's research workspaces, one insight recurs: authenticity outlasts trends and captures hearts. Audiences describe a sea of sameness and reward content that feels like it comes from someone with a point of view. SOMIN, an AI audience-research platform and our technology partner, reads these signals from real posts rather than surveys, which matters because people describe sameness in their own blunt words.

This aligns with what we have seen since 2020. The pieces that last are rarely the ones riding a format. They are the ones that say something specific in a recognisable voice. We explored why in why AI content all sounds the same.

How do you build a narrative that AI can serve?

1. Write the narrative in one paragraph

What you believe, who you serve, what tension you resolve for them. If it does not fit in a paragraph, it is not yet clear enough to guide a model.

2. Define three recurring themes

Themes are the territories your content returns to. They should come from the overlap between what you stand for and what your audience actually worries about. Audience evidence keeps this honest; SOMIN strategy is built for mapping those tensions.

3. Encode it in your voice system and prompts

Every prompt in your library should reference the narrative and themes, so even quick content pulls in the same direction. See our guide to prompt libraries.

How do you decide which trends to join?

Run every trend through a short filter:

  1. Fit: does it connect to one of our themes?
  2. Angle: can we say something only we would say with it?
  3. Audience: does our audience actually engage with this, or just the wider internet?
  4. Voice: can we do it without abandoning how we sound?
  5. Afterlife: will this piece still make sense in three months?

Two or more "no" answers means skip it. That is not a loss; it is a choice that protects recognition.

Be a trendsetter, not a trend chaser: let your narrative decide which waves are worth catching.

A worked example

A sportswear brand sees a viral "day in my life" format spreading. Its narrative centres on everyday athletes who train around full-time jobs. The filter says yes on fit, angle and audience: a pre-dawn routine from a real customer fits the story perfectly. A week later, a meme format with no connection to training appears. The filter says no. The brand sits it out, and nobody notices, because its content that week is about something its audience cares about.

Checklist: narrative health

  • One-paragraph narrative, agreed and written down
  • Three themes linked to audience tensions
  • Narrative referenced in the voice system and prompt library
  • Trend filter used before production
  • Quarterly check of themes against fresh audience evidence

Definitions

  • Brand narrative: the consistent story that gives content direction.
  • Theme: a recurring territory of content within the narrative.
  • Trend filter: a short set of questions that decides whether a trend serves the narrative.

Visibility without sameness

There is a practical upside too. As AI answers increasingly mediate discovery, consistent, specific content on a narrow set of themes helps search engines and AI systems understand what a brand is about. Our sister agency SNMRush works on exactly that visibility, in classic search and in AI answers. The Exostride case study is good further reading on grounding a brand's story in audience insight.

Trends will keep coming, faster than ever. A narrative is what lets you pick the right ones and stay recognisable while you do.

How do you measure whether a narrative is working?

Trend content is easy to measure in the short term: a spike in views. Narrative content needs a longer view. Useful signals include whether audiences start using your phrases back to you in comments, whether returning engagement from the same people grows over time, and whether people can describe what you stand for without prompting.

Listening is the most direct way to see this. If audience conversation about your brand begins to echo your themes, the narrative is landing. If it only mentions your trend posts, it is not. Review these signals quarterly alongside your normal performance metrics, and resist judging the narrative on any single week's numbers.

What if leadership wants the trend anyway?

It happens. Share the filter results openly and propose a version of the trend that does pass, perhaps with a different angle or a real customer at its centre. Most of the time the conversation shifts from whether to join to how to join in a way only your brand could. When it does not, do it once, measure it against narrative content over the following month, and let the evidence guide the next decision rather than opinion on either side.

Frequently asked questions

Should brands ignore trends?

No. Use trends when they fit your narrative and audience, and skip them when they do not. The test is whether the trend lets you say something only you would say.

How does AI make trend chasing worse?

AI makes it very cheap to produce trend content quickly, so many brands jump on the same format at once. Speed increases while distinctiveness falls.

What is a brand narrative?

The consistent story of what a brand believes, who it serves and why it exists. It gives individual pieces of content a shared direction and makes the voice recognisable over time.
